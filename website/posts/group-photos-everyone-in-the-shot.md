Every big group photo ends one of three ways. Someone's missing because they took the picture, or everyone holds a frozen smile through the 10-second-timer sprint, or a stranger takes one blurry photo and hands the phone back. There's a fourth way. Put your iPhone on a ledge or a tripod, stand with your group, and take the photo from a second device while you watch a live preview of the shot.

## The setup

1. Install [Remote Shutter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-shutter/id633274861) on two Apple devices. Your iPhone is the camera, and the remote can be a friend's iPhone, an iPad, an Apple Watch, or a Mac.
2. Prop or mount the camera phone. A pocket tripod is ideal, but a ledge, a fence post, or a books-and-water-glass rig works too.
3. Connect and pick roles. The devices find each other directly, so no Wi-Fi network is needed, and this works at the beach, at a trailhead, or in a wedding garden with zero bars.
4. Join your group and frame from the remote. The live preview shows exactly what the camera sees, so you wave people left, fix the framing, and then shoot. Take ten, they're free.

## Why this beats the alternatives

Versus the self-timer, there's no sprint and no guessing. You see the frame at the moment you shoot, so heads aren't cropped and eyes are open, and you can take many shots without jogging back to the phone after each one.

Versus handing your phone to a stranger, you keep your phone, you do the framing, and you get as many takes as you want.

Versus a Bluetooth clicker, a $10 button fires the shutter but shows you nothing, so you still can't tell if everyone's in frame. The live preview is the difference between hoping and knowing. There's more on that in [our Bluetooth remote comparison](/blog/iphone-camera-remote-without-bluetooth).

Versus the Apple Watch built-in Camera Remote, that one is genuinely good for quick shots and worth using if you have a Watch, but the preview is small and the camera has to be your own paired iPhone. With Remote Shutter any two Apple devices pair up, so the person with the best camera phone doesn't have to be the photographer.

## Three tricks for better group shots

Shoot a burst of moments, not one pose. You're not rationing timer runs, so capture the laugh after the posed shot, which is usually the keeper.

Go wide and high. Set the camera phone slightly above eye level and use the wide lens for rows of people, and you can switch lenses from the remote.

Check the corners. On the preview, glance at the frame edges for photobombers and cut-off elbows before you press the button.

Remote Shutter is a free download with no account and no subscription. Install it on both devices and you're shooting in about two minutes.
