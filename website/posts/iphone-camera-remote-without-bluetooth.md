Those $10 Bluetooth shutter buttons have three problems I keep running into. They pair like it's 2012 and un-pair again after an iOS update. The coin-cell battery is dead right when you reach for it. And the big one, they can't show you the shot, so you press the button blind and walk over to check.

If you have a second Apple device, you can skip the clicker. [Remote Shutter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-shutter/id633274861) turns an iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, or Apple-silicon Mac into a full camera remote for your iPhone, with a live preview of the frame.

## What "full remote" means

A Bluetooth button gives you one thing, a shutter press. A second device gives you the whole camera.

You see a live preview, so you know exactly what the camera sees before you shoot. You can take photos and start and stop video from the remote, not just snap. You can switch lenses, flip front and back, and toggle the flash and torch from where you're standing. And there's no pairing ritual, because the devices find each other directly and there's nothing to put in pairing mode.

## Setting it up

1. Install Remote Shutter on both devices. It's a free download, no account.
2. Open the app on both. They connect directly over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, so no router or Wi-Fi network is needed.
3. Pick which device is the camera and which is the remote.
4. Frame on the preview, then shoot photos or roll video from wherever you're standing.

## When a Bluetooth button is still the right tool

I'll be fair to the clicker. It's smaller than a second phone, it costs $10, and it clips to a selfie stick. If all you need is to fire the shutter while holding the phone at arm's length, a button is fine. The app wins when the phone is away from you, on a tripod, across the room, or up on a shelf, because then you need to see the frame, not just trigger it. There's a full comparison in [Remote Shutter vs. Bluetooth remotes vs. Apple Watch](/blog/camera-remote-comparison).

## No second device handy?

Two built-in fallbacks are worth knowing. The iPhone self-timer (Camera, then the arrow, then timer) gives you no preview at the moment of the shot, but it's reliable. And if you have an Apple Watch, Apple's built-in Camera Remote app gives you a small preview of your own paired iPhone. Both are fine for quick shots. The two-device setup is what you move to when you care about framing.
