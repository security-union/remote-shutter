If you have more than one iPhone in the house, you already own a multi-camera rig. Install [Remote Shutter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-shutter/id633274861) on every phone, pick one as the director, and the rest become cameras. You see every angle live in a grid on the director, and one button starts and stops recording on all of them at the same instant.

## What multicam does

The director connects to up to four cameras directly over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, with no router and no account. It shows a grid of the live previews, so you frame each phone before you roll, and you can tap any tile to see it full screen.

The shutter is one button for the whole rig. Photo or video, the capture fires on every camera at the same instant, so there's no more clapping for sync. Each camera records at full resolution to its own photo library, then the clips collect to the director, stamped with alignment metadata so any editor lines the angles up. I've tried it with iMovie, Final Cut, DaVinci Resolve and CapCut.

You set the camera settings once, from the director. Frame rate (24, 30 or 60), resolution (1080p or 4K), aspect ratio, HDR and tap-to-focus all apply to every phone, so the whole rig rolls at the same 24p or 4K without you walking around to each one.

Two cameras are free, and Pro takes it to four.

## What it is good for

For interviews and podcasts, run a wide two-shot plus a close-up on each person and cut between them later. For music and dance, one wide, one on the hands or feet, one on the face, recorded in a single take. For sports and skills, put a front and a side view on the same swing, lift or trick. Unboxings and tutorials want an overhead on the table and a face camera, started together. And for events, you can put spare phones on tripods around the room and direct from your own.

## How to set it up

1. Install Remote Shutter on every iPhone and open it on all of them. They find each other automatically, so keep the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radios on. No network is needed.
2. On the phone you will hold, choose remote. When two or more cameras connect, the director grid appears.
3. Mount the camera phones (any [tripod or clamp](/gear) works) and frame each angle from the director's live tiles.
4. Tap the shutter to take a photo on every camera at once, or start recording on all of them. Stop, and the clips collect to the director.

## Tips

Keep the cameras within about 50 ft (15 m) of the director with line of sight. If you need more range, put every phone on the same Wi-Fi network.

Set focus on each angle before rolling so the cuts match. Tap the preview where you want it sharp.

Old phones are fine as cameras. The director does the most work, so use your newest phone for that.

Turn on camera standby on the camera phones for long shoots. The screen rests while the phone keeps streaming and recording.

Single camera is still the everyday case, either a [spare iPhone as a remote camera](/blog/use-old-iphone-as-remote-camera) or an [iPad or Mac as the monitor](/blog/iphone-as-second-camera-mac-monitor). Multicam is the same app with more phones connected.
