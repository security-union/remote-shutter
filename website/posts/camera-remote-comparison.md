There are exactly three ways to fire your iPhone's camera without touching it: a Bluetooth clicker, the Apple Watch's built-in Camera Remote, or an app that turns a second Apple device into the remote. Each one is the right answer for a different job, so let me walk through all three.

## Bluetooth shutter button (~$10)

It's a coin-cell keychain button that pairs as a Bluetooth keyboard and sends a volume-press to snap the shutter. It's the cheapest option, tiny, clips to a selfie stick, and needs no second device.

The catch is it shows you nothing. No preview, no framing, no focus check, so you shoot blind and walk over to inspect. Range is a nominal 10 m and degrades through walls. Pairing gets flaky after iOS updates, and the battery is dead right when you finally need it.

So the right job is selfie sticks and arm's-length shots where you can already see the screen.

## Apple Watch Camera Remote (built in, free)

It's the stock Watch app that previews and triggers your own paired iPhone's camera. It's already on your wrist, with zero setup, a small live preview, a 3-second timer, and lens and flash control.

The limit is that it only controls the iPhone it's paired to, never a spare phone, a family member's phone, or an iPad. The preview is wrist-sized, fine for "am I in frame" and useless for real composition. It's photo-first, not a video director's tool. Here's a [deeper dive on the Watch remote](/blog/apple-watch-camera-remote).

So the right job is quick solo or family stills with your own propped-up iPhone.

## Second-device app: Remote Shutter (free download)

[Remote Shutter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-shutter/id633274861) runs on both devices. One becomes the camera, the other a full remote with a live full-screen preview, photo capture, video start and stop, lens switching, front/back flip, flash and torch.

It's the only option where you see the actual frame at full size before you shoot. It works between any two Apple devices, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, or Apple-silicon Mac, so a spare phone becomes a wireless camera and an iPad becomes a director's monitor. It connects peer to peer with no router or Wi-Fi network and no account, at about 50 ft line-of-sight range, or your whole Wi-Fi network's coverage. Nothing uploads anywhere.

You need a second Apple device with the app, and a mounted camera phone should be on power for long sessions. Video recording and the full control set are one-time paid add-ons, and free covers preview and photo capture.

So the right job is everything where the camera is away from you. Group photos with everyone in the shot, overhead or product video rigs, wildlife watching, form-check recordings, and old-phone-as-remote-camera setups.

## The one-line decision rule

Can you already see the phone's screen when you shoot? Then a button or the Watch is enough. If the phone is somewhere you can't see it from, the preview is the feature, and a two-device app is the tool. Most people end up combining them, Remote Shutter for the framing and the Watch as a handy extra trigger.
