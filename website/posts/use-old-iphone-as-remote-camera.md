If you have an old iPhone in a drawer, you already own a wireless remote camera. Install [Remote Shutter](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/remote-shutter/id633274861) on the old phone and on the one you carry, and the old one becomes a camera you can see through and trigger from up to 50 feet away. No router, no account, no monthly fee.

## What you need

- Your everyday iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, or Apple-silicon Mac as the remote
- A spare iPhone as the camera
- Remote Shutter installed on both, so the devices find each other and connect directly, peer to peer

Because the connection is device to device, this works in a forest, a garage, or a basement with zero bars and no Wi-Fi network.

## Set it up in two minutes

1. Install Remote Shutter on both devices. It's the same app on each, and you pick the role when they connect.
2. Open the app on both phones. They discover each other automatically over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi or your local network.
3. Make the old phone the camera. Choose the camera role on the spare phone and the remote role on yours.
4. Position the camera phone and walk away. From the remote you get a live preview of what the camera sees, and you can switch lenses, flip between front and back, toggle the flash, and capture photos or video.

Photos and videos save on the camera phone, so you can AirDrop them to your main phone when you're done.

## Things people actually do with this

Backyard wildlife. Set the camera phone near a feeder or a trail, watch the live preview from inside the house, and fire the shutter when something shows up. A motion-triggered trail cam decides for you, but here you frame the shot and pick the moment.

Checking on a room. Point the spare phone at the front door, a print job on the workbench, or a sleeping pet, and glance at the live view from the couch. Everything stays on your devices, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Impossible angles. Clamp the old phone above a basketball hoop, inside a bookshelf, or at ground level in the garden. Angles you'd never hold a phone at get easy once the phone doesn't need you behind it.

Self-recording. Film your golf swing, your deadlift form, or a guitar take with the camera across the room and the preview in your hand, so you're not walking back and forth to check framing.

## Tips for longer sessions

The live preview uses more battery on the camera side than on the remote, so a small power bank keeps an old phone shooting all afternoon. Any cheap phone tripod or clamp works for mounting; see the [field kit we shoot with](/gear) for what we use. And old batteries are fine here. A phone with 70% battery health that dies in an hour on its own runs indefinitely on a charger.

Remote Shutter is free to download, and the basics, live preview and remote photo capture, work without paying anything. One-time upgrades add video recording and the full feature set, and there's no subscription.
